Understandably, I wasn't in a very good mood in the following months. But (more unusually), something had happened to Anton too. After we stopped talking about the phenomenon, their smile had vanished too, and their face now looked pale, like mine. I was used to such changes: Anton often jumped from states of purest joy to mind-boggling despair, the change always instantaneous and easily noticeable, as if someone were switching their moods with a remote. But lately the remote was permanently switched to the "DEPRESSION" channel. Their constant changing of sexual partners had stopped, along with the constant stream of funny stories it generated. What was left was only the substance. The alcohol, that is...


Once when I came to see them, I saw them sitting on the sidewalk as if they were waiting for something to happen. I wanted to ask them immediately what was happening, but instead I sat next to them and waited for them to speak first:

"Engineer. Didn't you support the theory of evolution?"
{: .voice-curious}

"I find it meaningful, yes."
{: .voice-x}

I said it cautiously, expecting them to explode, but instead, they only got annoyed:

"Of course you find it meaningful, but I'm asking something else: Do you think it applies now. To us humans. That the world we inhabit at the moment is one big progress machine, driven by the 'fittest'?"
{: .voice-curious}

"And what if it is?"
{: .voice-x}

"Well, nothing, I was just wondering what my role is in such a world?!"
{: .voice-curious}

"Well, it's simple. So, when the circumstances around you change, you adapt to them, because otherwise you'll die. In a way, the driver is your self-preservation instinct, or in other words, fear of death. Because that's what the self-preservation instinct is—fear of death, right?"
{: .voice-x}

"And?"
{: .voice-curious}

"Well, okay, now imagine some guy like me, who isn't afraid of death, and lives as he WANTS, not as is best for him? If we're just a more talkative version of animals, where is THAT PERSON'S place in the picture?"
{: .voice-curious}

It struck me how they were hiding under the expression "that person."

"Well, aren't you afraid of death?"
{: .voice-x}

"I have no idea. Stop asking questions."
{: .voice-curious}

"Sorry. That's probably the only thing I know how to do."
{: .voice-x}

But, as usual, Anton had a point: why fight the machine? You cannot hurt the machine, you cannot make the machine surrender in fear (as machines don't fear), you cannot kill it (because it is not alive), nor enslave it so that it does what you want, because it cannot do what you want since it does not UNDERSTAND what you want. So, the only thing you can do is live with it. It is like a game in which you are forever trying to escape the robots that are chasing you. So, if you don't want to play the game, then what would you do?

This was the question that I thought about while waking up on a certain Saturday morning which felt just like any other Saturday morning in the last couple of months, or indeed just like any other day, as I was working on weekends too. I couldn't find an answer. Work was dull, but I couldn't think of anything else that I could do. Curious was in deep depression and I didn't want to talk to them, as talking to them made me sad as well, my other friends were also doing their own things... And so, I did the only other thing that I knew how to do with a whole day—I opened my laptop and I delved into my projects from work.

A few weeks earlier, I had been at my first performance review with the owner of the company, a guy named Todor Strugački. Formally, we also had a board of directors, but, as I heard, they never vetoed his decisions. And why would they—he had been managing the company for more than twenty years already, from the moment he was 25 and founded it until then, when he was 45. I had learned that all that happened with my rise was because *he* had liked me for some reason, and so I was very nervous about him finding out how mediocre I really was and (in my worst predictions) exposing me as the fraud I kinda was.

Before the review, I wished that I could explain to him what had happened to me when I wrote the initial version, and why he was wrong to hope that it would happen again. I wished to tell him something like: "Look, don't imagine my ability (if it can even be called that) is like yours. You know the problems you might encounter along the way. I don't. You are in full control of the situation—I can't even control myself. I don't know where I'm going, nor if I'll get there or not. The fact that I wrote that code was an impulse that appears and disappears, like the headache that causes it."

But when the meeting came, I realized that nothing could have been more out of place. The meeting defied all my predictions, both bad and good—he neither criticized me nor acknowledged my effort. As I found out much later, he had no WAY of criticizing or finding out that I was incompetent, as he was even LESS competent than me—he hadn't programmed in 20 years and he hadn't worked a full day in at least 10. His impressions of how good I was at my job were based only on what he heard from Anhela, who knew even less than he did.

So, there was no way for him and Anhela to know what I was doing and how I was doing it. This is why, shortly after the meeting started, the two of them interrupted my presentation, blurted a few suggestions about the work we were doing and concentrated on the part that really interested them—deadlines. This was the part where they did have an opinion—neither of them was happy. They wanted all the stuff, whatever it was, to happen faster. This was the part where they DID criticize me. They told me off for being too nice to my colleagues.

At the end, Strugački started talking in a friendly tone of voice.

"Hey, you seem tense. Relax, this is just a game."
{: .voice-strugacki}

And he winked at me. This wink looked like it provided a sort of resolution to the conflict caused by Anton's earlier speech. What he meant, although he obviously wouldn't put it this way, was: "Yes, you already see what the situation is with us, with our whole society: the machine is deathless, it is invincible, but it is not going to do anything to you, you see? In fact, you may get from it things that are actually quite good. All it takes is for you to follow some simple rules, and I already saw that you can do that. The situation is also kinda funny when you look at it that way. You don't need to do anything, just realizing what the situation is would make you infinitely better off than 99% of people, who do not."

This was the first time I had seen someone from the work universe as having something in common with people from my usual social circle. The first time that I saw someone at the office being distinctly... human. Being authentic, even more authentic than people like Anton, for whom authenticity was their whole persona. In one sentence, the man showed me a path forward that would allow me to do away with both my financial difficulties and my existential problems, as you may call them. All I had to do was stop taking myself so seriously.

At the end of the meeting I experienced a great sense of relief... However, it was not appropriate at all; as a matter of fact, this was precisely the time when I should have STARTED worrying.

From this meeting onwards, Strugački became something like a friend to me. We chatted a lot about different stuff, even personal things. He even did me some favors—showed me how to find my way around the real estate market, as I had already accumulated enough savings to afford a small flat. But the downside of this was that he was asking for more and more of my time. Whatever I was doing, I had to explain it to him and recalibrate based on his expectations. And we had conversations all the time, including when I was at Anton's house, where I visited frequently because I needed to relax with a beer in hand in the middle of a long day, and because I could afford a taxi home at any time of the night.

But, as I hinted, I rarely relaxed—Strugački was chatting with me all the time, as if he wanted to make sure I'd never take my mind off work, because, of course, that was all he wanted from me (although he sometimes pretended to care how I was). "Work, work, work..." he used to say. But the word "work" was too vague. What I was actually doing was managing HIS people, making HIS clients happy, making HIM richer. A fact that, as you might suspect, Anton never forgot to mention.

So, my situation was a bit funny, if you have the sense of irony: I would sit tired on Anton's couch, stare at the spot on the wall where the TV would normally go (Anton, of course, didn't have one) and try to finish my beer before Strugački inevitably called, while Anton (they probably would deny that this happened like this, but don't believe them) would make fun of me and call me a "bootlicker", to use a non-explicit version of the insult, while their friends, who were again throwing parties all over the place, laughed so hard that they spilled their drinks on the floor. And everything I tried to say to them triggered an even bigger tsunami of laughter. Every joke that I tried to make to get back to them was met with silence, as if all of them were members of a pact, made especially to exclude me.

There were days when I was sorry for them. There were days when I envied them and wished I were in their situation. But all the time I knew that ALL of us were crippled, all of us were robbed of that mythic vital force that was once the source of our happiness.

At the moment we left school, in the hangover after our prom night—or at least at the moment when *I* left it (here I am speaking mainly for myself, as, for example, Curious had stopped going to school way before the prom)—we found that it (the vital force) was just not there. The feeling was so weird for me, so unexpected... part of me was gone, as if I woke up one morning to find that my nose had run away, as Gogol once described.

We knew full well that we could not even hope to have it come back. So we tried to replace it with something: they with something called "irony", and I with something called "work". The issue was that both of these things were far different from, and far worse than, what they were advertised to be, and both were pretty lousy replacements for anything, and so we had nothing to do but laugh at each other, just as a person who replaced their nose with a carrot laughs at one who replaced it with a cucumber.

Both of these were basically a variation of "Don't take yourself too seriously". Like, with Strugački it was "Don't take yourself too seriously, just make money!", and with Curious it was "Don't take yourself too seriously..." well, I actually don't know what exactly Curious's philosophy was (I believe Curious was either the greatest genius that ever lived or totally deranged).

So, let's talk about something simpler—Strugački (and my colleagues). I came to understand their philosophy very well in the following months. It (the philosophy) was quite simple, and it was even easier for me to understand and adopt, especially since I worked and lived with people who followed it (sorry, I got so caught up in describing all these work-related (and life-related) worries in the previous chapters that I forgot to tell you that I had started dating Anhela a few months ago. But that's a story for the next chapter). Their only aim in life was not to be fools, to not allow other people to use them. For example, if Strugački was in his car and there was a street one meter shorter than the one he was taking, he would take it. Even if the street was forbidden, even if he was in the way of an ambulance, as long as he could get away with it, he would take it.

Their only aim was not to allow other people to use them... and they achieved it by trying to use other people as much as possible. By trying to seize every opportunity that they had (and to find a way to multiply those opportunities).

This was a philosophy that was very alluring, as it allowed you to do everything you wanted. But it was also very conformist, even if it didn't look like it at first sight, as it was all about other people and their perception of you. Strugački was always comparing himself with other people in order to determine whether he was a fool. If there was someone who did something before him, which was seen by people as beneficial, he would get mad and would rush to do the same thing. If he was hurt by someone, he would HAVE to take revenge, even if the thing was minor and the revenge was not worth it, simply because he couldn't afford to look like a fool in the eyes of OTHER PEOPLE (even if that meant becoming a fool in his own eyes).

Let's take the example with the shortcut. As a result of what I said before, if someone who is driving in front of Strugački takes a shortcut, then Strugački would HAVE to take it too. Even if the shortcut ran through a sidewalk next to a school... Or through a pile of shit... Or through Strugački's mother's grave. He would have to take it just because SOMEONE ELSE did. See the conformism: you are only what they say you are.

I once actually told a version of this to Strugački, when we were drinking coffee with him at the break, and he laughed and said that his philosophy could not be conformist (and was even the exact opposite of conformism), since it had turned him into part of the establishment, which by definition cannot be conformist, i.e. the person dictating cannot be a conformist.

But actually, it is not hard to see that the two things—conformism and the establishment—are closely interrelated: you cannot have conformists without an establishment, and you cannot have an establishment without conformists. I think that the dream of every conformist is to become the establishment, so they can dictate what everybody (including themselves) can do. This is the only position in which a conformist can be who they want to be, i.e. a conformist would change the whole world, just so they can be who they want to be. While remaining a conformist. 

All this was funny, but the tragic thing was that it looked like there was no other thing that you can be, when you grow up. You could be smug about your level of understanding... you could just be passive, or demonstatively individualistic, but all those tricks wore out quickly. If only we KNEW how to truly not be conformists, if only Anton knew how to just sit on their couch undisturbed and mind their own business, if only I knew what else could I do than than opening my laptop on a Saturday morning and spending my day looking at the screen, perhaps I could live.
